# FF14 Interior Design Brief — Sunset Woodland Retreat

## Design Objective

Design an original, cozy woodland-inspired interior for Final Fantasy XIV housing.

The space should feel like a warm, lived-in countryside retreat, combining a traditional wooden cottage, a quiet library, and an intimate residential lounge.

Draw inspiration from the reference's atmosphere, material palette, lighting, and spatial composition, but **do not recreate its exact floor plan, furniture arrangement, or architectural details**.

Prioritize visual quality, architectural coherence, functional zoning, and a strong sense of atmosphere over filling every available space.

## 1. Design Style

- **Primary style:** Rustic Woodland Cottage
- **Secondary influences:** English countryside interior, vintage library, cozy cabin
- **Mood:** Warm, intimate, peaceful, nostalgic, lived-in
- **Time of day:** Late afternoon to sunset
- **Visual identity:** Natural wood, rich textures, layered greenery, soft amber light

Avoid excessively modern, industrial, futuristic, or overly luxurious elements.

## 2. Color and Material Palette

Use a cohesive palette featuring:

- Warm walnut, chestnut, and russet-brown wood as dominant materials
- Muted forest green, deep teal, or sage as secondary accent colors
- Cream, beige, and warm ivory fabrics to provide contrast
- Small amounts of brass, aged metal, natural stone, and brick
- Rich but restrained colors in books, plants, and decorative accessories

Preserve material consistency across walls, flooring, furniture, and architectural details.

Avoid large areas of cold white, high-saturation colors, or unrelated materials.

## 3. Spatial Composition

Divide the interior into functional zones appropriate to the available room dimensions.

Potential zones include:

- A comfortable primary lounge or reading area
- A secondary study, library, or writing corner
- A compact kitchen or refreshment area
- An optional private resting area

Use architectural elements, shelving, lighting, and furniture orientation to distinguish zones without unnecessarily closing off the space.

Maintain clear circulation routes and natural transitions between areas.

The layout should feel intentionally designed rather than randomly decorated.

## 4. Primary Visual Feature

Create one memorable architectural focal point that evokes the feeling of being surrounded by nature.

Possible interpretations include:

- A deep window alcove with built-in seating
- A reading corner overlooking a forest
- A partially enclosed conservatory
- A warm sitting area illuminated by sunset
- An intimate garden-facing breakfast nook

Use existing window, partition, vegetation, lighting, and scenic furnishings to construct this feature where appropriate.

If necessary, combine multiple existing furnishings to simulate architectural elements or outdoor scenery.

The focal point should feel integrated into the room rather than appear as an isolated decorative object.

## 5. Furniture Selection and Placement

Select furniture exclusively from the provided FF14 furniture catalog.

Favor:

- Wooden cabinets, bookcases, and integrated storage
- Comfortable upholstered seating
- Small wooden tables and desks
- Window benches and multifunctional furnishings
- Warm lighting fixtures
- Indoor plants, books, framed artwork, and everyday objects

Combine furniture into coherent groups based on function, scale, and visual compatibility.

Use varied furniture heights and depths to create visual layering.

Leave intentional negative space around important architectural features.

Avoid excessive duplication, clutter, or decorations without a clear purpose.

## 6. Lighting and Atmosphere

Use warm, layered lighting to establish a calm late-afternoon atmosphere.

- Emphasize soft sunlight near windows
- Use localized warm lamps around seating and work areas
- Create gentle contrast between illuminated focal points and darker wooden surfaces
- Avoid uniformly bright illumination
- Preserve sufficient visibility of important furniture and circulation routes

Lighting should reinforce the spatial hierarchy and make the room feel comfortable and inhabited.

## 7. Decorative Detail

Use decorations to communicate a sense of daily life.

Include small clusters of books, plants, pottery, cushions, artwork, and personal objects.

Group decorative objects intentionally rather than scattering them evenly throughout the room.

Allow modest asymmetry and natural variation.

Decorative density should be higher near functional activity areas and lower along circulation routes.

## 8. Originality Requirements

The reference is a source of design principles, not a layout template.

Do not directly reproduce:

- The exact bay-window geometry
- The same seating arrangement
- The same cabinet and shelf configuration
- The same distribution of decorative objects
- The same camera composition

Develop an original layout that achieves a comparable level of warmth, craftsmanship, and visual cohesion.

Explore alternative focal points, room proportions, architectural compositions, and furniture combinations.

## 9. Implementation Constraints

- Use only furniture available in the supplied catalog.
- Respect the actual room geometry and furniture placement limits.
- Preserve usable routes between essential areas.
- Avoid unintended furniture collisions or visual artifacts.
- Intentional furniture overlap is permitted for architectural compositions.
- Prefer practical, reusable furniture combinations over unnecessary complexity.
- Do not invent furniture IDs, dimensions, or placement capabilities.

## 10. Design Evaluation

After generating the layout, render it from multiple useful viewpoints.

Evaluate:

1. Overall visual coherence and atmosphere
2. Clarity and quality of the primary focal point
3. Balance of furniture mass and negative space
4. Consistency of colors and materials
5. Naturalness of furniture grouping
6. Lighting quality and visual depth
7. Functional accessibility
8. Originality relative to the reference
9. Quality of furniture combinations
10. Composition from both entrance and occupied viewpoints

Identify the most significant weaknesses, modify the layout, and re-render.

Prefer meaningful improvements over adding more decorative objects.

**Final goal:** Create a distinctive, believable woodland retreat that captures the emotional warmth and sophistication of the reference without copying its specific design.
